Your own effects

Recipes — the custom transitions

A recipe is a named look: real Premiere effects stacked with keyframed parameters, saved under a word you choose. Say the word, it lands on the clip. Say what's wrong with it, it changes. These six are the starting set.

punch-in

Move

Slow scale ramp across the clip. The workhorse for talking-head ad cuts — stops a static shot feeling dead.

punch-cut

Move

Hard jump to tighter framing at the cut, no ramp. For the beat where the claim lands.

fade-up

Open

Opacity lift off black. Cleaner than a dissolve on an ad's first frame.

whip-blur

Transition

Directional blur spike at the cut — the fake whip pan. Reads as energy without matched camera motion.

beat-flash

Music

One-frame lift on the beat. Stacked across a drop, this is what makes a cut feel married to the track.

shake

Music

Position jitter for handheld feel on a locked-off shot. Tight amplitude, alternating keys.

What a recipe is not. A true Premiere effect plugin — a new GPU filter that shows up in the Effects panel — is a compiled C++ build against Adobe's native SDK. That is a different kind of project, and almost never what's actually wanted.

A recipe reaches the same screen result by stacking effects Premiere already has and animating their parameters. The difference that matters: a compiled plugin is frozen until someone rebuilds it, and a recipe changes when you say make the push slower.

The leverage

Why this is worth the build

Ads

  • Variation stops being labour. One body on the timeline, six hook heads placed, trimmed and exported from a single read of the sequence.
  • The teardown lands as an edit. A cut sheet already comes out of the teardown chain — it can now be executed onto a real timeline instead of an ffmpeg render.
  • You still get to touch it. Everything arrives as a normal Premiere edit: undoable, draggable, yours to fix by hand.

Music videos

  • Cutting to a track becomes arithmetic. Beats become markers, markers become cut points, and the whole sequence is placed in one pass.
  • Looks are repeatable. The treatment that worked on one section is a recipe applied across forty clips, not forty manual keyframe passes.
  • Volume without the craft tax. The part you'd never sit and hand-calibrate is exactly the part that's now programmed.
