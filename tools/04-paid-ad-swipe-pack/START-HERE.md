# Start here — the paid ad swipe

You have probably saved a competitor's video before because it was good. This
pack is about a different kind of saving, and a different reason.

---

## Organic tells you what people watch. Paid tells you what someone is buying.

An organic post — a normal video on a normal account — earns its views. If a
million people watched it, a million people *chose* to. That is worth knowing.
It tells you what holds attention.

A paid ad is not chosen. It is placed. Somebody wrote it, somebody approved
it, and somebody is spending real money every day to push it in front of
people who did not ask for it. That money is the signal.

Here is why that matters more than it sounds.

**Ad money moves fast and it does not stay put.** Nobody funds a losing ad out
of loyalty. If an ad is not making money back, it gets switched off within
days — sometimes hours. So an ad that is still running has survived a test you
never had to pay for.

**And it goes further than survival.** When a brand finds a pitch that works,
they do not just leave it running. They build twenty more versions of it. Then
fifty. Then three hundred. They pour everything into the thing that is
working, because that is where the return is.

Which gives you a reading you can trust:

> **The pitch carrying the most versions is the pitch making the most money.**

You do not need their sales numbers. You do not need anyone on the inside. You
just need to count. The brands in this pack did the expensive part —
discovering what converts in their market — and the shape of their ad accounts
tells you the answer for free.

One more thing organic cannot give you. An organic post is trying to be
liked. An ad is trying to be *bought from*. It has to name a problem, make a
promise, prove the promise, and ask for money — all in a few seconds, to a
stranger who was scrolling past. That is a much harder job, and the ads that
survive it are the closest thing to a finished template you will find.

---

## What this pack is built from

Four brands were swept — every ad each one was running, live, at one moment.
That is **2,626 individual ads** across **223 different pitches**.

The brands are not named anywhere in this pack, and neither are their
products. You will see them described only by who they sell to:

- a brand selling acne treatment to teenagers and their parents
- a supplement brand selling heart-health and gut-health capsules
- an Italian men's face-treatment brand
- a beauty brand selling foundation and serum to women 45+

Four brands, four completely different markets — which is the point. What
repeats across all four is not about skin or supplements. It is about how a
pitch is built.

---

## What is in here

**`what-the-spend-says.md`** — the strategy read. How much of each brand's
budget sits on how few pitches, how many versions they build of a winner, how
much is video versus still image, and what all of that says about how they
run. Every claim in it carries the number behind it.

**`angle-shapes/`** — ten files, one per pitch shape that showed up again and
again across these brands.

A **shape** is not a topic. It is the skeleton of the pitch: what the headline
does, what the first line of body copy does, what proof gets used, and what
the close asks for. Three brands selling three unrelated products can all be
running the same shape — and in this data, they are.

Each shape file gives you:

- the shape written out as a fill-in-the-blank template, with gaps in curly
  brackets like `{PRODUCT}`
- why it works on a human being
- how many ads sit on it, across how many of the four brands
- a worked example, written out in full for a made-up brand
- an image prompt and a motion note, so you can make a video version

---

## How to use it

**1. Read `what-the-spend-says.md` first.** Ten minutes. It changes what you
look for.

**2. Pick two or three shapes, not ten.** Start with the ones carrying the
most ads. Concentration is the lesson of this pack — do not immediately
ignore it by trying everything at once.

**3. Fill the template from your brand folder.** This is the whole job. Every
`{PLACEHOLDER}` is a question, and your brand folder already has the answer —
your customer, the words they actually use, your product, your offer. If you
have not built your brand folder yet, do that first. Nothing here works
without it.

**Fill in every single blank.** A gap you leave is a gap the model invents
something for, and it will invent something generic.

**4. Then run it through the teardown kit like anything else.** The shapes in
here are structure, not scripts. Once you have a filled-in shape, you feed it
through the same stages you already use — the teardown, the spec, the brand
injection, the hooks, the brief. What comes out is a shot-ready brief in your
brand's voice, not a competitor's ad with the names swapped.

**5. Make the video.** Every shape file ends with an image prompt and a motion
note. Generate the frame first, then move it.

---

## One rule, and it is not optional

**Never copy their words.** Not the headline, not a line of the body, not a
clever phrase you liked.

Two reasons, and both are real. It is their copy, and using it is a fast way
to a legal problem. And it will not work anyway — their words are tuned to
their product, their customer and their promise. Yours are different.

Take the shape. Leave the words. That is what every template in this pack is
for: the skeleton is the valuable part, and the skeleton is not theirs.
