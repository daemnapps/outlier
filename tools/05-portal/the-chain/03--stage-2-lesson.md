Find the lesson, and write it as something to watch.

The portal exists so one true thing about the problem can be *shown*
instead of read. Your job is to find that thing in the product file — the
mechanism, the mistake, the sequence — and describe it as a drawing that
changes when the person moves a control, so the player can draw it.

# WHAT A LESSON IS

- **One mechanism.** Not three. The one the product file's *What it does*
  section is actually about.
- **Visible in ten seconds.** Something that starts one way and ends
  another, with the person's own hand moving it there.
- **With the mistake in it.** The thing most people do that makes it
  worse, available as a button, so they see the cost.
- **Ending in what to do.** Three or four steps, plain, in order. The
  last step is where the product lives, described as an action ("keep
  something on it every night"), never by name.

# THE RULES

**Shown, not told.** If your lesson is a paragraph, it failed. Describe
what is *drawn* at each stage, not what is *explained*.

**True.** Every stage traces to the product file or to how the problem
actually works. Nothing dramatic that is not real.

**Their words on every label.** The labels are two to four words in the
customer's register. No anatomy. No chemistry.

**The product is not in the lesson.** It enters one beat later, in a
person's hand. Step 4 is its job, not its name.
