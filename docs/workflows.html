01  How it works

Video in, your
brief out

The engine runs the same five moves on anything you feed it. Step three is the only one that needs you — and you do it once.

STEP 01

Feed it a video

A competitor's ad, an organic post, a creator video someone sent you. If it already worked, it qualifies.

STEP 02

It reads every scene

Shot by shot: what is on screen, what is said, what they are wearing, where the beats land. Then it writes the spec you could rebuild it from.

STEP 03

It reads your brand folder

Your avatar, how they talk, what the product actually looks like, the objections, the offers. One folder, filled in once, read fresh on every run.

STEP 04

Your brand goes in

The structure stays; the content becomes yours. Hooks written off the source, your product placed where theirs was, the middle built out and closed.

STEP 05

Out comes the brief

Scenes, wardrobe, hooks, and a prompt for every frame — enough that an editor who has never seen your brand can cut it without asking you a question.

03  The folder everything reads

Set up
once

Every tool is brand-agnostic on purpose. They know how to tear a video apart and write a brief; they know nothing about you until they read this.

Fill it in once and it keeps paying: the same folder feeds the teardown chain, the swipe packs, and everything shipped after them.

Get the empty folder
  • core-avatars/<avatar>/profile.mdOne real person — what they have already tried, what they actually want, what makes them stop scrolling. A story name, never "males 18–24".
  • language/Their real sentences, verbatim, with a source on each. Plus the rules: how to write, and the words you may never use.
  • products/<product>.mdWhat it looks like to a camera — container, colour, finish, every word on the label. Skip this and a generated frame invents packaging that is not yours.
  • objection-bank.mdThe reasons people do not buy, in their words. Every script's middle answers one of these.
  • identity-anchors.mdWho and what may be named on camera. If it is not on the list, it does not go in an ad.
  • variables/video.mdThe map from a variable to a path. Already wired — leave it alone unless you move a file.

04  What to expect

Read this
before you
download

Honest about what this is: a system that does the thinking between a video that worked and a brief someone can shoot. It is worth your time if you are already making creative and losing to the blank page.

  • What it isA working process, not a button. You bring a video and a brand; it does the reading, the structuring and the writing — and tells you when it does not know something.
  • What you needSomething that can watch a video and read files. It was built against Claude Code. The prompts are plain Markdown, so any capable assistant can run them.
  • What it will not doInvent your product, your customer or your offer. Every gap in your brand folder shows up as a question, never as a confident guess.
  • The rule that makes it workRebuild the structure, never the content. A scalp treatment and a countertop demo can be the same format — that is the whole point.
  • Not shipped yetThe unattended runner — watch, run all thirteen stages, write the brief into a Google Doc. It needs API keys and a Drive connection, so it ships on its own.
  • What it costsNothing. MIT licensed, and it stays that way.

05  The production step

Where the scenes
get made

The chain ends with a prompt on every scene. Something has to turn that prompt into a shot — I use Higgsfield for that, and the prompts the chain writes are in its shape: an image prompt for the frame, a motion prompt for what the camera does.

Nothing here is locked to it. The prompts are plain description and work in any generator you already pay for.
